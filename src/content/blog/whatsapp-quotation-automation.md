The most expensive gap in a WhatsApp sales process is the one between **"what's the price?"** and the quotation actually reaching the customer.

It is rarely a pricing problem. It is that the person who can produce the quote is busy, the requirement is incomplete, and by the time someone types it out the customer has asked two competitors the same question.

## The requirement is the hard part, not the arithmetic

Teams usually try to automate the calculation first. That is the easy half. The real work is getting a complete requirement out of a customer who sends "rate batao" and nothing else.

A quotation needs specifics — size, quantity, material, variant, delivery location. A useful bot asks for exactly the missing ones, one at a time, and stops asking once it has enough. Two failure modes to avoid:

- **Interrogation.** Six questions in a row before any number appears. Customers drop off.
- **Guessing.** Filling in a default size to produce a quote faster. A confidently wrong price is worse than a delayed one.

The middle path: ask only for what genuinely changes the number, and quote as soon as you can.

## Show how the number was reached

A bare total invites haggling. A quotation that shows its working invites a decision.

If the price is per square foot, say the rate and the area. If GST applies, show it as a line, not folded into the total. If delivery and installation are quoted separately, say so rather than leaving the customer to assume they are included.

This matters more in a WhatsApp chat than in a formal PDF, because the chat message is what actually gets read. Many customers never open the attachment.

## Where automated quoting should not go

Be honest about the boundary. Automation works when the price is a function of inputs you can collect in a chat. It breaks when:

- The price depends on a site visit or physical inspection
- The job is genuinely custom and the "rate" is a negotiation
- A vendor has to quote you first — freight, specialised installation, imported components

For these, the right behaviour is not a guessed number. It is to say clearly that this item is priced on request, capture the requirement, and hand it to a human. A bot that says *"I'll get you an exact figure on this within the hour"* keeps the lead alive. A bot that invents a figure loses the customer twice — once at the quote, once at the correction.

## The follow-up is where most revenue is lost

Roughly speaking, a quotation that receives no follow-up is a quotation that was never sent. The customer read it, got busy, and moved on.

What works:

1. **Time it to the decision, not to your convenience.** A nudge a few hours later, then a day or two later, beats a burst of three in one evening.
2. **Reference the actual quote.** "Regarding the 10x12 cabin quote we sent" outperforms "just following up".
3. **Stop on a signal.** If the customer says they have decided or gone elsewhere, the sequence must end. Continuing is how you get reported and blocked.
4. **Respect the 24-hour window.** Outside it, WhatsApp requires an approved template — this is a platform rule, not a preference.

## What to measure

Two numbers tell you whether any of this is working:

- **Time from first message to quotation sent.** This is what automation is actually for.
- **Share of quotes that get a reply.** If this is low, the problem is the quote itself — usually an unexplained number — not the follow-up cadence.
